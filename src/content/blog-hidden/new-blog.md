The soundtrack to The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time is a certified banger. There's catchy melodies, lush string textures, boppy percussion. 
Maybe it was creativity through restriction. The Nintendo 64 combined processing of audio and graphics onto one chip meaning any extra overhead given to audio had to be carefully balanced with potential graphical bottleneck. With so little horsepower available to render compositions the core components really have to cut through. 
Maybe it was simply the fact that Ocarina of Time has music as a core game mechanic. So much of moving the plot of the game forward and even traversing time and space is triggered by playing a melody on the titular ocarina. As a player you collect melodies as though they are Mario's gold stars. 
Its place at the zenith of video game musicality is so firmly fixed that it's barely worth talking about. However, if you want to listen to 'Hyrule Field' or 'Inside the Deku Tree' where do you go? There's never been an official soundtrack release from Nintendo
Well if you want it in a totally unadulterated form there's archive.org. 
86 tracks - including "End Credits" "A", "B", "C" and "D" as well as the "Bolero of Fire" and the "Nocturne of Shadow". All pulled straight from the game. It's completely free to download. 
So, that's the answer, I guess... Job done. 
No, I don't think so. I want to hear the soundtrack rendered through modern technology. A faithful representation but a contemporary one. With a wider soundscape greater depth and clarity and more authentic, orchestral instrumentation. 
These three albums can all be had on Band Camp for relatively cheap as of the time of writing. They're all amazing for their own reasons. 
I've got three albums that I found and ended up purchasing. They represent three different approaches to video game music. One is an official certified, dare I say more proffesional, version. The other two are distinctly home-made, but no less brilliant. Dark Grunt https://dark-grunt.bandcamp.com/album/zelda-ocarina-of-time-orchestrated
A straightforward recreation of the tracks from the Ocarina of Time Game. This Album more than the other two gets at the itch I was trying to scratch in the first place. The production is a little more home-made but still incredibly skilful. If you want to be taken chronologically through the tracks with little to no deviation from the arrangements from the game this is it. 
The broadening of soundscape allowed by modern software instruments is very satisfying and it really does sound like "how it felt at the time"
